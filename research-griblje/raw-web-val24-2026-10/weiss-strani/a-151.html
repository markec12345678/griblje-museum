A depiction of the inhabitants of Metlika between the late Mediaeval and early Modern periods Neumarkt [New Market] was established on an important route connecting the lands in the very eastern reaches of the domain of the House of Görz. It came into being and began evolving in the last quarter of the 13 th century, when the core of the castle was erected on what would become the town plateau. It gave rise to the development of a brand new settlement below in a previously uninhabited area. Te earliest residents were subjects freed of their burdens hailing from the local area, the wider territory, and Metlika, as well as individuals who moved to the new attractive settlement from older urban settlements in the March and Carniola. Te latter brought town laws of Kostanjevica and began applying them as customary law. Even before 1323, the residents achieved the confrmation of town privileges in accordance with Kostanjevica law. Tis is how, in the course of a century-long Görz rule, Novi trg developed the fundamental characteristics of a mediaeval urban settlement. In addition to townsmen, the town population also included noble residents, which is attested by at least six existing noble houses in the 14 th and 15 th centuries as well as the presence of the Teutonic Order. In spite of a 1407 decree, expressly dictating equality of town burdens, the relations between the townspeople and the town’s nobility and subjects residing on their properties were turbulent. Tis was most particularly linked to unfair competition of craf and commerce conducted on exempt properties held by the nobility on town grounds. Immigration interdictions dating to the beginning of the 15 th century indicate that the town was attractive to rural population.

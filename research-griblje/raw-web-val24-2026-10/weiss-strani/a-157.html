Tese families had individual destinies, which are excellently recorded, unlike the Uskoki who shared a common fate and whose testimonies are much less well preserved. Tese migrations transformed the image of the town population and with it the character of Metlika, which was described by Valvasor at the end of the 17 th century. Afer the end of the 16 th century, the new territorial factor, the fort of Karlovac, certainly held greater appeal and presented better opportunities than Metlika. Consequently, it intercepted the potential infux of people from southern Slavonija or Croatia. Terefore, there the words of the town elders, who wrote in 1686 that no foreigner wishes to come to the town, but rather, he secretly departs, hold merit even though the frst half of the 17 th century saw a number of new bourgeois families, such as for example the De Fedrigi hailing from Italy attested in Metlika and Karlovac, the Verderbers and the Perrs from the nearby Kočevsko, the Bobners and Črnugeljs from Črnomelj. Demographically, Metlika was stagnant or even sufered negative trends that kept the town population lower than it was at the end of the 15 th or beginning of the 16 th century. Last names of the townspeople of Metlika encountered in the 17 th century are exceptions in the frst entirely credible surveys of the population and estates from 1738 and 1752, while the last names of the older families, even the most infuential, are entirely absent. From the time of its creation at the end of the 13 th century, through the peaks of its development in the 15 th , challenges of the 16 th and deterioration in the 17 th centuries the settlement known as Novi trg and Metlika displayed many faces and played many roles. For the House of Görz, the Frankopans, and the House of Cilli it was a signifcant town, a pivot point and fundament of their political ambitions. Te Habsburgs saw it as a military outpost of great importance protecting their territories and Croatia. Te town of Metlika held renown with the territorial prince, among the Landstände, Carniolans, and Croatians, as well as the hereditary enemy to the Christian name – the Ottomans, who broke their lances on its walls. It also received a place in the works of humanists and in treatises on ancient history and on the appearance of this part of Europe. In his widely read and signifcant work Italia antiqua (1624), Filip Clüver, the father of historical geography from the University of Leiden, stated that Metulum, the centre of ancient Iapodi where the later Roman Emperor Augustus almost lost his life during a siege, lay in the Slovenian March, on the lef bank of River Kolpa, usually called Metlika, prompting late scholastic debate. Hieronymus Megiser, the author of the Annales Carinthiae (1612), which was the frst history of the Territory of Carinthia, situated the clash between the backhanded pagan Droho and the Christian Duke Valjhun, son of Kajtimar there by the River Kolpa (on the borders with Croatia, where the Carniolan town of Metlika can be found today). Te townspeople of Metlika, however, most probably knew nothing of these great stories of the past that supposedly afected their town.

Te pressure on all parts of the economy, the devastation of the area, constant fear and uncertainty had a negative efect on the town’s demographic makeup, causing emigration of the older population in the 1520s. Te paucity of sources makes it impossible to quantify it conclusively, but it is possible to make a general estimate. Out of twenty-eight bourgeois families attested in the 15th century, only one, the Herlič family, can be identifed a century later. However, further understanding of sources will enable us to trace a number of families attested in the 16th century back a hundred years. None of the leading families reported to have held the position of town judge in the 16 th century appear among the town judges of the 17 th or 18 th century; this indicates that four centuries of various processes fundamentally reshaped the makeup of the town’s population. Tere are several reasons for this; in addition to impoverishment and natural extinction (familien abgestorben) mentioned by the townsmen themselves at the start of the 18 th century, one such process is certainly emigration. Te townsmen related extreme examples of this process at the end of 1528 in the time of severe Ottoman pressure; they wrote that they need to sell the cattle fom their house and court, run away fom their wives and children, and take to begging because this poor town is daily sinking ever deeper into ruin and desolation and so they estimated that within half a year’s time few townsmen or other people will remain, but they will for various purposes depart elsewhere. Te town families that were fnancially capable of relocating certainly did so; for example a branch of the wealthy Strobl family moved to the much safer Villach in Carinthia. A more detailed study of sources would surely reveal a series of families in inner Austrian towns, who sought out new opportunities outside the endangered land of Metlika. Following fnal incursions (1575, 1578) and the construction of the fort of Karlovac (1579), the town of Metlika no longer experienced such pronounced Otoman military pressure, though they were ofen burdened by the regularly assembling territorial and mercenary armies, particularly during the Long War (1593-1606); these were ofen supplied and maintained at the town’s own expense. In the 16 th century all social ranks throughout the land of Metlika underwent a transformation.
